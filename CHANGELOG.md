# Changelog

## 1.0.0 (to come)

klad is a drawing and animation app where every stroke stays editable, hours later too, and where an AI agent can draw in the same drawing beside you from the command line. 1.0 fills in what painters, illustrators, pixel artists and animators reach for every day:

- **Several drawings open**, with a tab each, and copy and paste between them. **Save a copy**, **Rename**, **Duplicate** and **Revert** in the File menu. ([Getting started](https://klad.studio/docs/guide/getting-started))
- **Perspective you paint along**: one, two or three vanishing points, rulers and ellipses the brush follows, a grid, guides and snapping, and straight lines and shapes with the brush. ([Painting](https://klad.studio/docs/guide/painting), [Shapes and text](https://klad.studio/docs/guide/shapes-and-text))
- **Your brushes in every drawing**, kept apart from any one drawing and shared as a file.
- **Selections that do more**: an ellipse, a wand by colour, cut, in front and behind, typed numbers, and pictures to and from other apps through the clipboard. ([Selecting](https://klad.studio/docs/guide/selecting))
- **The canvas and layers**: crop, trim, resize, turn and flip the whole drawing; merge layers; a right-click menu on every layer. ([Layers](https://klad.studio/docs/guide/layers))
- **Out to every app**: PNG, JPEG, WebP, TIFF, PSD with its layers, PDF with shapes kept sharp, SVG, video, GIF and animated WebP, and the selection alone; PSD and SVG come in too, and sprite sheets come in as frames. ([Exporting](https://klad.studio/docs/guide/exporting), [Animating](https://klad.studio/docs/guide/animating))
- **Rigs that bend cleanly**: a weight brush with add, remove, smooth and strength, and the weight under the pointer. ([Bones](https://klad.studio/docs/guide/bones))
- **Big canvases handled with care**: klad tells you before a canvas is too big for your machine, and never ends without a word when memory runs out.

Drawings are saved in format `klad/9`; `klad/8` drawings open unchanged, and klad 0.4 does not open `klad/9` drawings.

### Added

- **Agent apps without a terminal**: the first time klad opens, its welcome offers to connect the agent app you use, and File > Connect an agent app… does it later. One click puts klad into Claude Desktop, the Codex app, Claude Code, Cursor or VS Code, and they paint, read and edit your drawings through klad's MCP server (`klad mcp`, `klad mcp --install`). ([Working with an agent](https://klad.studio/docs/guide/with-an-agent))
- **Several drawings open at once**, with a tab each above the canvas: click a tab or press **ctrl Tab**; opening a drawing that is open shows its tab. Strokes and shapes copied in one drawing paste into another with their brushes, pictures and palette colours.
- **File > Save a copy…** (**shift cmd S**), **Rename…**, **Duplicate** and **Revert** (back to how the drawing was when you opened it, in one undo step); in the start window, right click a drawing to duplicate it or move it to the Trash (`klad save-as`, `klad rename`). A copy gets ids of its own.
- **Drawing guides**: perspective with one, two or three vanishing points, a ruler and an ellipse that the brush follows; **View > Drawing guides…** adds them and drags their points; **cmd** frees a stroke (`klad guides --perspective --ruler --ellipse`, `klad stroke --follow`).
- **Grid, guides and snapping**: a grid with its spacing, stronger lines, square or isometric look (**View > Grid…**, **cmd '**), rulers to drag guides out of (**cmd R**), and snapping of shapes, points, moves, the pen, straight strokes and bones to the grid, the guides and other shapes (**shift cmd '**; `klad grid`, `klad guides`).
- **Straight lines and figures with the brush**: **shift** click paints a straight line from the last stroke, **shift** drag keeps a stroke level, upright or at 45 degrees, and the brush paints lines, rectangles and ellipses (`klad stroke --line --rect --ellipse`).
- **Your brushes** in every drawing: save a brush from the brush editor or a right click; rename, group, move, forget and share them as one file. A drawing that uses one keeps its own copy (`klad brushes --save --library --take`).
- **Selections**: an **ellipse** and a **wand** with a tolerance next to box and loop, **alt** to take away (`klad lasso --ellipse --at --tolerance`); **cmd X** cuts; **cmd ]** and **cmd [** bring forward and send back (`klad order`); numbers for position, size and angle in the selection bar.
- **Transforming**: a corner scales from the opposite corner, **shift** frees the proportions, **alt** scales from the middle, **shift** keeps a move level and turns in 15 degree steps.
- **Pictures through the clipboard**: a picture copied in another app pastes onto a new layer or a pixel layer; **cmd C** puts a picture of the selection on the clipboard, and **shift cmd C** copies what you see in it (`klad import --clipboard`, `klad export --clipboard`).
- **Shapes**: the outline's width, ends and corners; copy a shape's look onto others (**alt cmd C**, **alt cmd V**, `klad style`); **Unite**, **Subtract**, **Intersect** and **Exclude** (`klad combine`); text to paths.
- **The canvas**: a canvas size window with an anchor, scaling the whole drawing, the paper colour, the crop tool (**C**, with handles; past the edges it grows the canvas), **Crop to selection**, **Trim**, turn and flip (`klad canvas --size --crop --trim --resize --turn --flip --paper`).
- **Layers**: **Merge down**, **Merge selected** and **Merge visible**, keeping strokes, pixels and shapes editable, **Ungroup**, and a right-click menu on every layer in the Layers pane and the timeline (`klad merge`, `klad ungroup`).
- **Exports**: JPEG, WebP (lossy, lossless and animated), TIFF (8 or 16 bits), PSD with a layer for each layer and groups, PDF with vector layers kept as shapes (a page per frame or clip, on the drawing's size or a paper), MP4 video, SVG, and the selection alone (`klad export --format jpeg|webp|tiff|psd|pdf|mp4|svg --only`).
- **Imports**: PSD from Photoshop, Krita and Affinity with its layers and groups; SVG as shapes; a sprite sheet cut into frames, or a folder of numbered pictures, onto a new layer with a clip (**File > Import frames…**, `klad import sheet.png --cell 32x32 --to art.klad`).
- **The weight brush**: paint, add, remove and smooth (**shift 1** to **4**), a strength that the pen's pressure drives, hardness, the weight under the pointer and **alt I** to pick it up, a legend and every bone's weight in its own colour (`klad weights --mode --strength --hardness --at`).
- **Very large canvases**: a canvas too big for the machine's memory is refused with the size it needs, a tight one is made with a warning, and the editor uses the graphics' real texture limit.

### Changed

- Opening a drawing keeps the one you were in open, in a tab, instead of closing it.
- The bone tool's weights bar: the mode buttons, then size and weight; strength, hardness, pressure, the legend, **automatic** and **smooth all** under **⋯**.
- The export window has a format row for pictures and its own pages for PDF and video; the GIF window also writes animated WebP.
- Drawings are saved in format `klad/9` (the grid, guides and drawing guides).
- The licence says plainly that the drawings you make are yours: you may sell them and use them in your own and your clients' commercial work.
- The Mac disk image opens to a window with klad, an arrow and the Applications folder.

### Fixed

- A fill bent by bones got stair-steps and spikes along its edge; its weight now reaches past its soft edge. Where little weight is painted, a place fades into its nearest bones instead of jumping between them.
- A pose, in-between or weights op in a batch with a fill is refused with what to do (a batch of its own after the drawing), instead of a message that sent you to the command you had run.
- Running out of memory ends with a message, which the editor shows the next time it starts, instead of the app stopping without a word.
- A canvas wider than 8192 px could not be shown by the editor; it now uses what the graphics can show and says so when a drawing is wider.
- On Windows, a drawing whose folder is renamed while it is open is followed, as on the Mac and Linux.
- A new layer made while a layer inside a hidden group was picked was hidden with the group, so nothing could be painted on it; it now goes above the hidden group.
- Going to the layer above or below past the top or the bottom, and pasting a look onto the shape it came from, now say why nothing happened.
- Moving strokes in a large drawing follows the pen again: while you drag, klad moves a picture of the strokes and draws them in their new place when you let go.
- Select all (cmd A) no longer picks the eraser marks that ride on strokes, so deleting everything it picked works instead of naming a mark that was already gone.
- The brush's **more** menu and the selection's numbers stay open while you pick and type in them; the numbers button says **numbers**, and duplicate has its own icon next to copy.
- With **all layers** on, the wand selects on whatever layer is painted under the click, and select all takes the strokes of every layer.
- On the Mac, the pointer no longer stays stuck as a resize arrow after you put the pen down straight from a number field or a panel edge.
- A brush taken out of your brushes in one drawing no longer gives an error in another drawing that still listed it; that drawing keeps its own copy.
- Merging layers painted with lock alpha no longer lets the strokes of one layer paint over the other: each stroke stays on the paint of the layer it came from, so the picture looks as it did before the merge.
- Moving everything on a layer (select all, then drag) follows the pen however many strokes it holds, alpha-locked and thick oil strokes too, and lands about three times faster when you let go.
- Select all frames in a drawing with no layers left closed klad; it now says to add a paint or vector layer first.
- `klad merge` of large layers lists the first few strokes it copied instead of thousands, so an agent's answer stays short; `--json` still lists every one.

## 0.4.0 (2026-11-19)

The editor after a full audit: every tool, command and window was walked through the way a person uses them, and what fell short is fixed.

What is new:

- **It fits a small screen.** On a 1440 by 900 window the canvas went from a third of the window to more than half: the timeline folds for stills, the panels share the height, menus and windows fit, and **Hide panels** (Tab) leaves a button to bring them back.
- **It says what it does.** Plain messages without internal ids. A tool on a locked or hidden layer says so, and a command that cannot run says why.
- **Keys follow what you work in.** Click a frame and **cmd C**, **V**, **D**, **Delete** and **A** act on frames; press on the canvas and they act on strokes again. **cmd +** and **cmd -** zoom, **cmd 0** fits, **cmd ←** and **cmd →** go to the first and the last frame.
- **Export by kind.** **File > Export** has an entry for each kind: image, frames, sprite sheet, GIF, Aseprite file and Godot. Each window shows only what that kind uses and says where the file will go.
- **Selections.** **S** drags a box, or a loop. A right click opens copy, paste, duplicate, delete and **Move to new layer**. Shapes copy and paste, the selection's colour picker holds the palette and recent colours, and turned boxes stay turned.
- **Colour.** Mix with **HSV**, **HSL**, **RGB** or **Lab** sliders beside the wheel.
- **Text.** Select inside a text while you type it; **cmd Z** takes back the typing in one step. On a pixel layer, text becomes hard-edged pixels (`klad pixel --text` from the command line).
- **Pixel art and animation.** A starter palette of eight ramps, new frames on ones, drags in the timeline that select, repeating the selected frames, a paste on a held frame that gives the frame its own drawing, a pencil that answers to the pen's tilt, and an eraser with its own size.
- **Any tool on any layer.** The brush and the fill on a vector layer paint on a new paint layer above it; the pen and the shape tool on a paint layer make a vector layer above it.
- **For agents.** `klad apply --steps` makes each line of a script an edit of its own, paced with `--delay`, so a drawing can be watched as it appears. The editor names the agent's changes, and undo says whose step it takes back.
- **Safer with an agent beside you.** The editor reads the drawing before it writes and never writes over a change it has not read; a drawing whose folder is renamed while it is open is followed.
- The brush no longer tapers the strokes you draw by hand unless you ask for it.

Drawings are saved in format `klad/8`, as in 0.3.0; older drawings open unchanged.

- The editor no longer writes over what an agent changed a moment before. Every change now reads the folder first when it has changed, and nothing is written over a change klad has not read.
- Fixed the canvas after undoing **Make it a map layer** on a layer that was no longer selected: the layer's paint stayed off the canvas until another layer was picked. **Make it a map layer** and the **map** button are one undo step each, with the light maps they turn on.
- Fixed links that did nothing: **Help > Guides**, **What's new**, the links in **About klad** and in the start window, and the report and support emails now open with the system.

## 0.3.0 (2026-11-16)

Light maps for 2D lighting: normal, height, specular, emission, depth and occlusion maps made from what is drawn, shaped by hand, written beside every export and lit in Godot. Drawings are saved in format `klad/8`; older drawings open unchanged.

- Fixed opening a drawing on the Mac: **Choose a .klad file...** in the start window (it said folder) and **File > Open…** could not pick a `.klad` drawing, which Finder shows as one file. The open panel now takes a `.klad` drawing; other files are greyed out.
- Fixed shade and rim on posed layers. The shade and rim fills that `klad light` makes keep the part of the shape outside a cut, and a pose turned the fill with its bone but left that cut where it was in the rest drawing, so in posed frames and their in-betweens the rim grew into a wide crescent and the shade slid away. Posing now moves the cut with the fill, in `klad apply` poses, in-betweens and the editor alike; a frame posed before this comes right when you pose it again.
- Fixed cuts in in-betweens. A fill or stroke cut by a lasso kept its cut where it was in the first key when an in-between moved it: flood fills between keys, and marks that ride a line (marks drawn by hand between keys and marks found in one key only). The cut now moves with the mark.
- Fixed hard edges on posed fills. A pose sampled each fill at the nearest pixel, so a turned fill got a stepped edge and thin bands along it broke into dots; it now blends between pixels as `klad transform` does, and a pose and a transform of the same turn give the same picture. Frames posed before this keep their old edge until they are posed again.
- Colour squares open the colour picker. A colour square outside the Colour panel (an effect's colour, a fill or gradient stop, the shine and glow colours, the preview lights, the fog, the grey around the canvas, a new drawing's paper) opens the colour wheel with a hex field and **use the current colour**, instead of taking the current colour on a click; dragging on the wheel is one undo step. A glow colour is removed with **remove** in its picker.
- Light maps for 2D lighting: a normal map (OpenGL style, green up, as Godot expects), a height map, a specular map and an emission map, made from what is drawn. The drawing's surface rounds in from its outline by a bevel (**round**, **chamfer**, **pillow** or **flat**; **fit** beside it, or `bevel=fit`, makes it as deep as the shape so the whole silhouette rounds like a ball), rises where paint with body is thick, and follows height layers you paint (white raises, black lowers); a top layer can have a surface of its own, and layers can paint the normal, specular and emission maps directly. Map layers are a layer type of their own: **map** in Layers adds one, **Layer > Make it a map layer** and **Make it a colour layer** convert, the list shows each with an icon for what it draws, and its Layer settings hold only what applies (what it draws, the range for height); from the command line `klad add art.klad layer map height`, and `klad ls` lists them as map layers with what they draw (`draws height`). Map layers never show in the picture, so while one is selected the canvas shows its own map (height, normal, specular or emission) and goes back to the picture on another layer. **Window > Light maps** turns them on and holds the settings (opening it in a full dock folds other panels to make room, and says which), and turning them off and on there brings back the surface and the lights they had; **View > Next light map** (**M**) shows each map over the canvas, **View > Lit** shows the drawing lit by its preview lights, and holding **L** lights it with the pointer. From the command line: `klad maps art.klad --on`, `klad set art.klad l7 map=height`, `klad set art.klad g2 surface.bevel=12`, `klad lights art.klad --add point --at 120,80`, `klad render art.klad --map normal` and `--lit`, `klad hash art.klad --map normal`. Drawings are saved in format `klad/8`; older drawings open unchanged.
- Shaping the light maps by hand. On a height layer any brush paints height, white raising and black lowering: while it is selected the canvas shows the height map with the stroke under way drawn over it, the Colour panel shows a grey ramp and five steps from **lower** to **raise** with how many px the colour moves the surface, choosing the role picks white, and **white raises** beside it sets the range; from the command line a stroke or fill in a colour on a height layer prints what its lightness does. On a normal layer the brush paints a direction: pick it on the sphere in the Colour panel (the middle faces out, **flatten** goes back to it, the row under it holds the eight leans), or turn on **direction from pen tilt** and lean the pen the way the surface faces. From the command line: `klad stroke art.klad l9 --normal nw` (or `--normal 0.5,-0.3`), and `{"normal":"nw"}` in a stroke op.
- Shine and glow. A layer's **shine** (0 matte to 1) and the colour of its shine make the specular map, and **emissive** makes it glow at full brightness whatever the light (windows, eyes, fire); for parts of a layer, **+ the current colour** beside it makes only the parts in that colour glow, on every frame, with no layer of their own; shading blended over a glowing part (multiply and the like) lets its glow through, a normal layer over it covers it; all of it is in a colour layer's **Layer settings** under **Light**, with **a surface of its own**, and the light preview shows the highlights and the glow. From the command line: `klad set art.klad l3 shine=0.6 specular=#ffd0a0`, `klad set art.klad l5 emissive=true`, `klad set art.klad l5 glows=#bff2ff,#ffd040`.
- Depth and occlusion maps. The depth map is near white: each plane's depth, a layer's **z** (px nearer within its plane) and the height; it is written as 16-bit grey, `--invert` for far white. The occlusion map darkens creases and the foot of raised forms. **M** now shows them too. From the command line: `klad render art.klad --map depth`, `--map occlusion`, `klad set art.klad l3 z=12`.
- Light maps in every export. `klad export art.klad --maps normal,specular` (or `all`) writes each map beside the PNG, the frames or the sheet pages, with the same size, trim and layout, named like the picture with `_n`, `_h`, `_s`, `_e`, `_d` or `_o`; normal maps are written as plain data with no colour profile. The export window has a tick for each map.
- A sample for light maps, **lit-creature**: the walking creature and a basketball on one drawing, lit for a game's 2D lights, with the creature's parts raised and recessed on a height layer that follows every frame, eyes that glow by colour and a ball rounded by a fitted bevel.
- Lit sprites in Godot. With the maps on, `klad export --godot` (and the editor's **export to Godot**) writes the normal and specular maps and makes every sheet page a `CanvasTexture`, so `PointLight2D` and `DirectionalLight2D` light the sprite; what glows plays on a child sprite that ignores the light; `art_lit.tscn` holds the sprite in front of a plain backdrop that catches the light and the shadows (a light inside the sprite, like a lantern's own, is not shadowed by it: the mouse light checks as it moves), the drawing's preview lights (one follows the mouse) and its ambient. Parallax scenes, rigs and tilesets get the maps too.
- Shadows from the silhouette. `--occluders` traces the outline of every distinct picture into an occluder that switches with the frame, so the sprite casts a shadow; rigs get one per part, parallax scenes one per plane. **shadows** in the export window, **View > Overlays > Occluders** and `klad render --overlay occluders` show the shapes. The lights in `<name>_lit.tscn` cast soft shadows that start right at the outline (filter PCF5, smoothing 1: Godot blurs 2D shadows by angle from the light, so more smoothing spreads them past the sprite's edge).
- Shader samples. `--shaders fog,rim,occlusion` adds a short shader that reads the maps (fog towards a colour by depth, a rim light from the height, darker creases) to the sprite or the parallax planes, a start for your own.
- Light maps for pixel art. A pixel drawing's maps step by whole pixels and stay sharp at any export scale, with the normals taken from a normal palette of 9 directions (or 17: `klad surface sprite.klad --directions 17`); the Colour panel shows the palette on a normal layer, and `klad pixel sprite.klad l2 --normal nw` paints one. `klad surface` sets a layer's own surface or the drawing's.
- Checking the light maps. `klad check art.klad --maps --all` and **View > Check the light maps** report normals pointing into the surface, layers that read flat under light, map paint where nothing is drawn, glow past the drawing and maps that jump between frames while the picture holds still; the Light maps panel lists them and a click goes to the place. A new sample, `samples/lit-lantern`, builds the lantern with its maps, lights and a Godot export.

## 0.2.0 (2026-11-12)

Pixel art: pixel layers in the colours of a palette, tools that draw whole pixels, animation, tiles, checks and exports that stay sharp. Drawings are saved in format `klad/7`; older drawings open unchanged.

- Pixel layers, the start of pixel art in klad. A pixel drawing (**Pixel art** in the start window, or `klad new sprite.klad --pixel 64x64`) is a small drawing of whole pixels with no soft edges; **Layer > New pixel layer** or `klad add art.klad layer pixel` adds a pixel layer to any drawing. `klad pixel sprite.klad l1 --color skin --cells '4-11,5-9'` puts pixels on it as one mark bound to a palette colour (a colour the palette lacks is added), and `--erase` clears them. Each mark stays editable: recolour it with `klad set`, move it with `klad transform`, remove it with `klad rm`; `klad strokes` lists them. Exports above full size keep the pixels square. Brush strokes and fills stay off pixel layers. The format is now `klad/7` (stroke files version 3); older drawings open unchanged.
- A sharp view for pixel drawings. In the editor the zoom steps in whole pixels, so every pixel is the same size; a grid shows between the pixels once you are zoomed in (**View > Pixel grid**), the pixel under the pointer is outlined and its position shows in the status line, and the **Real size** panel shows the drawing as the player sees it, at 1x, 2x or 4x. `klad render` enlarges a pixel drawing to about 512 px, and `--overlay grid` numbers its pixels along the top and the left.
- The palette of a pixel drawing. Changing a palette colour changes every pixel painted with it: click the colour in the palette, change it, and press **update** (or `klad palette art.klad --recolour skin --to '#e8b796'`). **palettes** adds the colours of a palette that comes with klad (PICO-8, Resurrect 64, four greens, 1-bit; `klad palettes` lists them) or of a palette file, a list of hex colours or a GIMP palette (`klad palette art.klad --load pico-8`). **ramp** adds five steps of the current colour, from dark and cool to light and warm (`klad palette art.klad --ramp wood --base '#8b5a2b'`), and `--ramp skin --of skin-dark,skin,skin-light` groups colours the palette has.
- The pixel pencil and eraser. On a pixel layer the brush tool (**B**) draws whole pixels in the current colour, which joins the palette, and the eraser (**E**, or the pen's eraser end) clears them; each drag of the pencil is one mark, and the eraser takes its pixels out of the marks under it and leaves none of its own, so pixels you move there later stay whole. A one-pixel line is pixel perfect (no doubled corners), the size is a square of whole pixels (**[** and **]**), and shift click draws a straight line from the last pixel. From the command line, `klad pixel` takes `--path` (SVG path data as a line of pixels) and `--size`.
- Sprites as text, for agents. `klad pixel sprite.klad l1 --grid sprite.txt` draws a sprite from a legend (`i = ink`) and rows of characters, one per pixel, each character becoming one mark bound to its palette colour; `klad pixels sprite.klad l1` reads a pixel layer back the same way, with its legend and numbered rows and columns (`--plain` in the form `--grid` reads, `--json`).
- Shapes and fill on the grid. On a pixel layer the fill tool (**G**) fills every pixel joined to the one you click that has exactly its colour (**across corners**, **read all pixel layers**), and the shape tool (**U**) drags a rectangle, an ellipse or a line of whole pixels, outlined or **filled**. From the command line: `klad pixel` with `--line`, `--rect`, `--ellipse` (`--filled`) and `--fill x,y` (`--diagonal`, `--ref`).
- Selecting and editing pixels. On a pixel layer the stroke tool (**S**) drags a box, or a loop, around pixels and takes them, cutting marks between whole pixels; drag them to move by whole pixels, scale by whole numbers or turn by quarters, and the bar under them flips, turns, recolours (exactly the current colour), copies and deletes. **Select same colour** takes every mark in the selected colours. From the command line: `klad lasso` takes `--box x,y,w,h`, `klad strokes --entry skin` lists one colour's marks, and `klad recolour` gives pixel marks the exact palette colour.
- Shading pixels. The pixel pencil, the shape tool and the fill tool have three inks: **colour**, **lighter** and **darker**; the last two step the pixels you go over along their colour's ramp. **dither** draws a share of the pixels in an even pattern, with the **second colour** for the rest. **Layer > Outline the pixels** adds a one-pixel outline in the current colour, and **Lighter** and **Darker** in the bar under selected marks step them along their ramp. From the command line: `klad pixel --shade`, `--dither` with `--second`, `--outline` (`--inside`), `klad recolour --step`, and `klad recolour --from skin --to skin-dark` to replace a colour.
- Mirror and tiles for pixel art. The pixel pencil, shape and fill tools draw each pixel's twin across the **mirror** line. **View > Tile: wrap at the edges** makes the drawing a tile: what you draw past an edge comes back in on the other side, fills go round the edges, and the canvas shows its repeats around it. From the command line: `klad pixel --mirror-x 16` (and `--mirror-y`), `klad new tile.klad --pixel 16 --wrap`, `klad canvas tile.klad --wrap on`, and `klad render --repeat 3` to see a tile repeated.
- Animating pixel layers. Frames, holds, links, clips and onion skins work on pixel layers as on paint layers. A keyed pixel layer moves by whole pixels and stays sharp. In-betweens, poses, weights and bones refuse pixel layers and say why: they move points and bend strokes, so draw the frames or key the layer's position.
- Exports that stay sharp, and GIFs. A pixel drawing exports at whole-number sizes only, up to 64 times (`klad export sprite.klad --scale 8`), enlarged pixel for pixel; the export window shows **times real size** and the size in pixels. Sheets, numbered frames and Godot exports follow the same rule, and the Godot scenes of a pixel drawing turn texture filtering off. New for every drawing: `klad export art.klad --gif` and **GIF** in the export window write an animated GIF for each clip, or one of the whole timeline, with each frame's own duration. A GIF holds 255 colours: `--dither` (the **dither** box) mixes them in a fine pattern so the soft gradients of a painting do not band.
- Palette variants. A drawing can hold other colours for the same sprite, like a blue team beside a red one. In the Colour panel **new variant** makes one and shows it; colours you change while it is shown are its own, and the palette itself keeps its colours. Pick **base** or a variant under the palette to show it. The export window exports what is shown, one variant, or **every variant** in one go. From the command line: `klad palette --new-variant blue`, `--variant base`, `--rename-variant`, `--rm-variant`, and `klad export --variant blue` or `--variants`.
- Aseprite files in and out. `klad import hero.aseprite` opens an Aseprite file as a klad drawing: its layers and groups, every frame's drawing, linked cels, the palette and its tags as clips. In the editor, **Choose an Aseprite file...** in the start window (or its path pasted there) does the same and opens the drawing it made. `klad export hero.klad --aseprite`, or **Aseprite** in the export window, writes a drawing's pixel layers back to an Aseprite file. klad says what it leaves out each way.
- Pictures to pixels. **File > Pixels of a picture…** turns a PNG or JPEG into pixels on the pixel layer you are on, in the palette's colours, or makes a pixel drawing of the layer you are on or of the whole picture at a size you choose. From the command line: `klad pixelize sprite.klad --image ref.png --to l1`, `klad pixelize hero.klad --size 64`, and `klad import ref.png` for a new pixel drawing from a picture. Each pixel becomes part of a mark bound to a palette colour, so the result is edited like any pixel drawing.
- Tilesets. **View > Tileset: cut into tiles…** cuts a drawing into tiles of one size; the canvas shows the tile lines, and **as a tile set** in the export window writes a Godot TileSet with a tile for every cell that is not empty. From the command line: `klad new ground.klad --pixel 64x48 --tile 16`, `klad canvas --tile 16`, `klad pixels --tile 2,0` and `klad pixel --grid --tile 2,0` to read and draw one tile at a time, and `klad export --tileset <folder>`.
- Pixel checks. `klad check sprite.klad` lists the usual slips of pixel art with their places: single stray pixels, doubled corners in one-pixel lines, jagged lines, banding, and colours that are not in the palette. In the editor **Check the pixels** marks them on the canvas and follows your edits until you turn it off. They are places to look at, not errors.
- A third agent skill, `klad skill pixel`: the craft of pixel art (size and palette, silhouette, clusters, clean lines, outlines, ramps with hue shift, shading without pillow shading or banding, anti-aliasing by hand, tiles, and a checklist at real size), and animating a sprite frame by frame: the four poses of a walk, heights and holds, what moves with the body and what trails it, the idle, and how to judge it. Under the MIT licence, like the other two.
- Two pixel art samples, `sprout` (a 32 px character with an idle and a walk) and `ground` (a tileset of 16 px tiles), in the start window and `klad samples`, and a guide, Drawing pixel art: starting a drawing, drawing and mirroring, moving pixels, ramps, a second colouring, animating a sprite, turning a picture into pixels, the checks, tiles and exports.
- `klad check` is quiet enough to read: it lists what stands out (a single pixel outside the shape or in a flat area, an L in a drawn line) and counts single pixels among other colours and corners in shading in one line as left out. A 64 px sprite that gave 77 findings gives 15. The editor's **Check the pixels** marks the same ones.
- `klad export -o` takes a folder that exists for every kind of export and puts the export in it under its usual name; a sheet used to land next to the folder.
